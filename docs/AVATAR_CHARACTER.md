# A character for every avatar (DRAFT, for the owner's approval)

The owner's ask from the start: each avatar has its own nature, and its tap animation shows it ("one crosses arms, one
smiles, one grumpy face, each with its own nature"). Today all animations do the same smile, tilt and blink. This sheet
gives each of the 30 a nature and **one distinct 2 s gesture**. **Nothing is generated until the owner approves it**;
the cap then comes from the lead's held budget (D). Revised after the lead's review.

Contact sheet for the phone: `docs/mock/avatars.html` (the two framing options, then the 30 stills with the nature and
gesture under each, in Hebrew).

## Rules

- **No nature follows from gender, skin, glasses, size or clothes.** The kids' natures were reassigned for that; "shy"
  went to an animal (the hedgehog).
- **No two natures and no two gestures alike**, and each gesture uses **a different body part or rhythm**, so they read
  apart at tile size (about 96 px). At most three are head-only movements (owl swivel, cat nose-up, music-lover nod);
  only the koala closes its eyes (and the fox winks).
- Kind: nothing mocking or scary; the grumpy one is funny-grumpy, never mean.
- Safe everywhere: no thumbs-up, no OK sign, no pointing.
- As before: no teeth, no tongue, no distortion, no outfit change, the same art style and framing, a static camera;
  ends on the still. Every frame is checked before use.
- Avoided because they fail: a turned cap, a hand at a cap brim, a fast salute, many octopus arms moving at once, wings
  opening, a hand over the face.

## The owner's decision: framing

**Option 1 (recommended): head and shoulders, as the approved portraits.** Gestures of the head, face and shoulders
only (shrug, eyebrows, cheeks, lean, turn, a look). No new stills; the portraits he approved stay exactly as they are.
The one thing it cannot show is hands, so "crossed arms" becomes the grumpy kid's puffed cheeks.
Cost: 29 new animations (only the fox's wink is kept) × 3.0 = **87.0**; head-and-shoulder moves fail less than hand
gestures, so plan about one retake in five: **about 105 at most**, with a cap of **90** if the hardest fall back to the
still and the CSS bob (as the owl has today).

**Option 2: waist up, with hands.** A new waist-up still for every character in the same style, then the gesture with
hands (crossed arms, a fist pump, "shh"). Cost: 30 stills × 0.5 = 15, with drift retakes **15 to 20**; then 29
animations, and hand gestures fail more often (about one in three): **87 + 29 ≈ 116**; **total about 105 to 135**,
likely **125**. Risks: each new still drifts from the approved portrait (face, hair, outfit), so the owner re-approves
30 stills; the picker tiles change framing (or the still and the animation no longer match); hands are the model's
weakest point.

**Recommendation: option 1.** It keeps the art he approved, costs less, and fails less; the characters' nature still
reads clearly from the face and shoulders.
